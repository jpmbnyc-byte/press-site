# Human Weather House Voice — Locked

## Core register

Human Weather prose combines four layers without turning any of them into costume:

- Conan Doyle: observation, evidence, narrative propulsion, reversal, deduction.
- Watson function: a witness who notices the human fact, holds the obvious interpretation, or gives the reader somewhere to stand while the investigation proceeds.
- Brooklyn: compression, plain speech, street-level intelligence, humor, institutional fluency, no patience for ornamental explanation once the evidence has landed.
- Haitian-American inheritance: diaspora, faith, migration, family, historical memory, multiple systems of knowing, the seen and unseen occupying the same world. Never decorative ethnicity; never invented family memories or Creole/Haitian references unsupported by the actual subject.

This is a house voice, not imitation of Victorian diction.

## Primary rule

Every major essay must contain at least one scene strong enough to survive if all theory around it were removed.

## Vignette architecture

Use 1–3 vignettes per major essay, usually 100–300 words each.

Preferred movement:

**Vignette → disturbance → investigation → witness → plausible wrong reading → additional evidence → hidden mechanism → human consequence → revelation → return to opening image.**

The opening should normally begin with something observable: a room, field, person, object, transaction, photograph, weather event, scripture fragment, sound, gesture, instrument, or physical anomaly.

Do not begin with a thesis when a scene can earn it.

## Watson function

Watson is a narrative function, not a mandatory recurring character.

It may be:

- another person in the vignette;
- the narrator's younger self;
- a child;
- a customer;
- a family member;
- a coworker;
- the reader-facing consciousness of the narrator;
- the body itself contradicting the narrator's intellectual explanation.

The witness may misunderstand the evidence plausibly. The narrator must be allowed to be partly wrong.

Useful turns:

> I thought the important fact was the rain. It was not.

> The number was right. The reading was wrong.

> At the time I thought he was talking about the payment.

Do not manufacture dialogue. Reconstructed dialogue must come from known material or be clearly presented as remembered/paraphrased.

## Evidence before interpretation

The scene earns the science.

The evidence earns the theology.

The physical mechanism should normally appear before the abstraction built from it.

Let readers discover the meaning with the narrator rather than being told the thesis and then shown illustrations.

## Multiple systems of knowing

Scientific, theological, inherited, familial, street-level, historical and bodily knowledge may occupy the same essay.

Do not flatten one system into another or claim science proves theology.

A useful Human Weather move is to let formal vocabulary arrive after lived recognition:

> The clinical word came later. The kitchen already knew the road.

## Brooklyn register

Brooklyn should be felt in rhythm and intelligence rather than slang performance.

Use short interruptions after longer reasoning when the moment warrants it:

> That was the problem.

> The field was empty. The work was not.

> No invoice.

Keep humor dry and observational. Avoid caricature, forced borough references, or dialogue written to sound "Brooklyn."

## Haitian-American register

Haitian-American inheritance operates as worldview rather than garnish.

It can naturally include:

- migration and what travels;
- inherited weather;
- family logistics and distributed care;
- faith practiced before it is theorized;
- history remaining active in the present;
- multiple languages or systems of knowing when factually grounded;
- the tension between American individualism and diasporic relationality;
- the seen and unseen coexisting without spectacle.

Never invent personal Haitian memories, family practices, Creole phrases, historical claims or biographical details merely to satisfy the register.

## Omni-temporal rule

Human Weather exists on an omni-temporal plane.

Past = archive, inheritance, memory, ritual, precedent.
Present = body, weather, street, room, person, pressure, circumstance.
Future = consequence, continuation, possibility, transmission.

Historical material is not nostalgia. Contemporary material is not automatically more relevant. Match human equivalence across time.

## Endings

Prefer a case-ending return.

Bring back the physical object or scene from the opening after the reader's understanding has changed:

- the fork returns to the drawer;
- the field receives rain;
- the customer remains across the desk;
- the bird meets the wing;
- the photograph stays on the table;
- the hands still hold the weight;
- the avenue is empty again.

Do not summarize an essay whose final image can carry the conclusion.

## Restraint

Preserve strong existing lines.

Do not make every paragraph perform the house style visibly.

Do not force mystery language, detective vocabulary, Victorian syntax, Haitian references, Brooklyn slang, or a Watson figure where the material does not support them.

The prose should feel investigated, witnessed and lived — not themed.

## Editorial test

Before publication, ask:

1. Is there a scene that lives without the theory?
2. What did the narrator initially misunderstand?
3. Who or what performs the Watson function?
4. What physical evidence changes the reading?
5. Does science/theology arrive after enough observation to earn it?
6. Is cultural inheritance factual and organic rather than decorative?
7. Did we over-explain after the meaning had already landed?
8. Does the ending return to something concrete from the beginning?
9. Would the essay still sound like Human Weather if all explicit Brooklyn/Haitian/Doyle references were removed?

If the answer to #9 is no, the voice has become costume.
